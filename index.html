Trailer, sewage, and which permit you need

You're planning a towed trailer for an Indian menu. Two rules decide almost everything downstream, and they cut in your favour:

  1. A trailer counts as a food truck. Utah Code 11-56-102 defines a food truck as a fully encased food service establishment “on a motor vehicle or on a trailer that a motor vehicle pulls.” Towed does not mean permanent.
  2. A mobile unit is allowed to connect to a sewer. Utah rule R392-102-7 lets a mobile food business drop the onboard water tank and use pressurized water — but only while “concurrently connected to a public sanitary sewer system in a manner approved by the local health officer.”

So connecting to a sewer does not automatically make you a permanent establishment. There's a middle path, and it's the one worth chasing.

Two caveats. The phrase “in a manner approved by the local health officer” means Salt Lake County has discretion — this must be confirmed by phone, not assumed. And the county's own mobile guidelines describe a mobile food unit as “vehicle-mounted,” which is narrower than the state definition. Ask before you buy.

What an Indian menu changes

Cuisine matters here more than it does for most concepts, and it pushes against Path A:

  • Water volume. Rinsing rice and dal, blanching, and washing a heavy pot load add up fast. Under Path A, roughly 30 gallons covers your entire service — handwashing, warewashing and prep combined.
  • Grease load. Samosas, pakoras, bhatura and tempering all shed oil. Wastewater tanks fill faster, and any sewer connection brings the FOG program and a grease interceptor with it.
  • Tandoor. If you want one, ask specifically. Solid-fuel versus gas changes the hood type, the fire suppression requirement and possibly whether it's allowed in a mobile unit at all. Ask the health department and the fire inspector before you buy.
  • Spice and turmeric staining is a finishes question on Path C, where surfaces must stay light-coloured, smooth and cleanable.

Measure a real service before you decide. Cook a full menu at a commissary and meter the water. If it runs well past 30 gallons, Path A is out — and finding that out now costs you an afternoon instead of a trailer build.

      How syncing handles two devices at once

      Changes are pushed about a second and a half after you stop typing, and again whenever you leave the tab. When you come back to a tab, it pulls first and merges.

      Merging is deliberate rather than blunt: for each step, whichever device edited it most recently wins on status, owner, date and cost — but notes are unioned, so a note you wrote on your phone and a note you wrote on your laptop both survive. Next-step items merge the same way, and deleting one is remembered so it doesn't reappear from the other device.
